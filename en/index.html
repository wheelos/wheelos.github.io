Build the project like a house ​

Autonomous driving is not a one-step black box. We recommend seven visible stages: application discovery, boundary design, vehicle and sensors, software and data, replay validation, pilot operation and expansion assessment. Each stage exposes inputs, outputs, metrics, risks and unresolved issues.

View the seven-stage delivery process, exit criteria and pilot metrics →

Product portfolio ​

The core product is the Integrated Autonomous Driving Kit, which combines compute, sensors, time synchronization, a baseline autonomy stack and developer tools. Site adaptation, calibration, maps, training and simulation are project services whose scope requires confirmation.

A faster route from prototype to real-world testing ​

Low-speed autonomous vehicle projects usually spend too much time aligning sensors, timestamps, drivers, coordinate frames, software modules and chassis interfaces. The WheelOS Integrated Autonomous Driving Kit turns those dependencies into a defined starting point.

01 / START

One reference architecture

Compute, sensors, time synchronization, data recording and autonomous-driving modules are organized around a shared interface.

02 / BUILD

Room for your own IP

Focus on your vehicle, site and algorithms while retaining access to APIs, data and standard CAN control interfaces.

03 / VALIDATE

Evidence you can reproduce

Use record-and-replay workflows, calibration tools and visible system state to investigate failures and compare changes.

Designed for controlled environments ​

The kit is intended for engineering evaluation and deployment in manufacturing, healthcare, retail, logistics and campus transport tasks, as well as other controlled or semi-controlled sites. Typical platforms include low-speed delivery, logistics, patrol, cleaning, vending and special-purpose vehicles.

Clear scope: The kit is not a certified production autonomous-driving system. Site risk assessment, regulatory approval and vehicle-level functional safety remain part of the customer project.

Verified knowledge and product scope stay separate ​

WheelOS uses three principles: AI-native retrieval, evidence-gated knowledge and explicit product boundaries. A Product specification describes configuration and delivery scope. Verified requires a reproducible evidence record with environment, metrics, raw results and review.
